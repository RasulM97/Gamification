# Founder Questions

Ten questions that genuinely require founder judgment — trade-offs the
simulation evidence cannot settle. Evidence-backed questions (e.g. "is
Help broken?") are deliberately excluded; the simulation answered those.

1. **How invisible should CVE be for employees?** Model 2 keeps a valuable
   optional dashboard; Model 3 makes CVE nearly headless. Where on that
   spectrum should the product sit — and is "employees never need to open
   CVE" a *goal* or a *failure of engagement* in your vision?

2. **What should the home screen fundamentally represent?** "What needs my
   attention" (action inbox), "how the company is flowing" (awareness), or
   "my work and my rewards" (personal)? Option B blends them; you may want
   one to dominate.

3. **How much incentive transparency should employees have?** Should an
   employee be able to see *why* a rule exists and what it pays before it
   pays them (full legibility, may invite gaming) — or only understand
   outcomes after the fact (current wallet-reason level)?

4. **Which delivery channel is acceptable first?** The recommendation is an
   abstract contract with email as the first adapter. If you believe your
   target companies live in Slack/Teams and email would be ignored, that
   changes the D1 pilot order — this is a market-knowledge call, not an
   engineering one.

5. **Should Help/Thanks/Recognition originate outside CVE at all?**
   D2-B says yes, via explicit channel actions. The alternative philosophy:
   appreciation *should* cost a deliberate visit so it stays meaningful.
   The simulation shows the visit kills usage; it cannot tell us whether
   friction-free appreciation devalues it culturally.

6. **What is the acceptable abuse perimeter for channel intake?** Explicit
   commands only (recommended) is safe but rigid; richer channel
   interaction (thread-based help lifecycle) is smoother but closer to
   inference. Where do you draw the line before WSE/AI?

7. **Is Finance/audit self-service a first-class goal?** D3-B and a weekly
   digest serve it; if your actual buyers don't include a finance observer,
   that scope can shrink. Who is the economic governance audience in your
   target companies?

8. **How strong is the anti-surveillance constraint relative to manager
   utility?** The D5-B guardrails (aggregates only, no rankings, no
   scores) are my reading of your principle. Confirm the boundary — e.g.
   may a manager see *their own team's* recognition/help counts per
   person, or is even that over the line?

9. **Task Lite: strategic fallback or future core?** D6-B positions it as
   fallback + listener. If your long-term vision includes CVE-native work
   management for small companies, that argues for keeping task UX
   investment alive (D6-B with a small-company track) rather than letting
   it atrophy.

10. **What evidence threshold should greenlight implementation?** Options:
    decide now on simulation evidence alone (fastest); decide now but gate
    build on a human UAT of clickable prototypes (safer); or run human UAT
    of the *current* product first to replicate the simulation findings
    (slowest, most rigorous). Your risk appetite sets the path.
